\section{Multidimensional knapsack interdiction proofs}\label{app:mkip}

We use the packing interdiction notation of Section~\ref{app:notation}: leader
rows $Gx\le g$, follower rows $Wy\le c$ with $m$ rows, $p,G,g,W,c\ge 0$ integral,
and $\Phi(x)$ the follower's optimum. Let
\[
  U(x)=\max\bigl\{p^{\top}y : Wy\le c,\ 0\le y\le\mathbf{1}-x\bigr\}
\]
be the value of the follower's linear programming (LP) relaxation, and
$p_{\max}=\max_jp_j$.

\subsection{The dualised follower LP}

\begin{lemma}[Dual form of $U$]\label{lem:mkip-dual}
For $x\in\{0,1\}^n$,
\[
  U(x)=\min_{\lambda\in\mathbf{R}^m_{\ge 0}}D(x,\lambda),\qquad
  D(x,\lambda)=c^{\top}\lambda+\sum_{j=1}^n\max\bigl(0,\,p_j-(W^{\top}\lambda)_j\bigr)(1-x_j),
\]
and the minimum is attained by some $\lambda$ with $0\le\lambda_k\le p_{\max}/w^{\min}_k$
for every row $k$, where $w^{\min}_k$ is the smallest positive entry of row $k$
of $W$ ($w^{\min}_k=1$ if the row is zero).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The LP is feasible ($y=0$, since $c\ge 0$) and bounded ($0\le y\le\mathbf{1}$), so by
LP duality $U(x)=\min\{c^{\top}\lambda+\sum_j(1-x_j)\nu_j : W^{\top}\lambda+\nu\ge p,\ \lambda,\nu\ge 0\}$,
and the minimum is attained. For fixed $\lambda$ the best feasible $\nu_j$ with
$x_j=0$ is $\max(0,p_j-(W^{\top}\lambda)_j)$; for $x_j=1$ the term has coefficient
$0$. This gives $U(x)=\min_\lambda D(x,\lambda)$. For the box: if
$\lambda_k>p_{\max}/w^{\min}_k$, lower $\lambda_k$ to $p_{\max}/w^{\min}_k$. Every
item $j$ with $W_{kj}>0$ still has $(W^{\top}\lambda)_j\ge\lambda_kW_{kj}\ge p_{\max}\ge p_j$,
so its term stays $0$; items with $W_{kj}=0$ are unaffected; and $c_k\lambda_k$
does not increase because $c_k\ge 0$. So $D$ does not increase.
\end{proof}
\noindent Not formalised.

\begin{proposition}[Exact linearisation]\label{prop:mkip-milp}
Let $U^{*}=\min\{U(x): Gx\le g,\ x\in\{0,1\}^n\}$. Then $U^{*}$ is the optimal
value of the MILP
\begin{align*}
  \min\quad & c^{\top}\lambda+\sum_{j:\,p_j>0}v_j\\
  \text{s.t.}\quad & v_j\ge p_j-(W^{\top}\lambda)_j-p_jx_j,\quad v_j\ge 0\quad (p_j>0),\\
  & 0\le\lambda_k\le p_{\max}/w^{\min}_k\quad (k=1,\dots,m),\qquad Gx\le g,\quad x\in\{0,1\}^n.
\end{align*}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $x_j\in\{0,1\}$ and $(W^{\top}\lambda)_j\ge 0$,
\[
  \max\bigl(0,p_j-(W^{\top}\lambda)_j\bigr)(1-x_j)=\max\bigl(0,\,p_j-(W^{\top}\lambda)_j-p_jx_j\bigr):
\]
both sides equal $\max(0,p_j-(W^{\top}\lambda)_j)$ when $x_j=0$, and both are $0$
when $x_j=1$.
For fixed $(x,\lambda)$ the smallest feasible $v_j$ is this maximum, and items
with $p_j=0$ contribute $0$. So the MILP's value is the minimum of
$D(x,\lambda)$ over leader-feasible $x$ and $\lambda$ in the box, which is
$\min_xU(x)=U^{*}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:mkip-dual}.
\end{proof}
\noindent Not formalised.

\subsection{The integrality-gap bracket}

\begin{lemma}[Integrality gap of a packing problem with $m$ rows]\label{lem:mkip-gap}
For every $x\in\{0,1\}^n$, with $p_{\max}(x)=\max\{p_j: x_j=0\}$ ($0$ if there is
no such $j$),
\[
  U(x)-m\,p_{\max}(x)\ \le\ \Phi(x)\ \le\ U(x).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\Phi(x)\le U(x)$ because the LP is a relaxation. The LP's feasible region
$P=\{y: Wy\le c,\ 0\le y_j\le 1-x_j\}$ is a nonempty polytope, so the LP has
an optimal vertex $y^{*}$. A vertex of $P\subseteq\mathbf{R}^n$ satisfies $n$
linearly independent constraints of $P$ with equality. Let
$F=\{j: 0<y^{*}_j<1\}$; these coordinates have $x_j=0$ and neither bound
tight. The bound constraints of coordinate $j$ are $y_j\ge 0$ and
$y_j\le 1-x_j$, with normals $\pm e_j$, so a linearly independent family of
tight constraints contains at most one bound constraint per coordinate
outside $F$ and none for coordinates in $F$. Hence at least $|F|$ of the $n$
independent tight constraints are rows of $W$, and $|F|\le m$. Let
$\tilde y_j=1$ if $y^{*}_j=1$ and $\tilde y_j=0$ otherwise. Then
$\tilde y\le y^{*}$, so $W\tilde y\le Wy^{*}\le c$ (as $W\ge 0$) and
$\tilde y\le\mathbf{1}-x$; $\tilde y$ is a binary follower response, and
$\Phi(x)\ge p^{\top}\tilde y=U(x)-\sum_{j\in F}p_jy^{*}_j\ge U(x)-m\,p_{\max}(x)$.
\end{proof}
\noindent Not formalised.

\begin{theorem}[Bracket]\label{thm:mkip-bracket}
Let $L\le U^{*}$ be any lower bound on $U^{*}$ (for example a MILP solver's
proven dual bound for the MILP of Proposition~\ref{prop:mkip-milp}) and let
$\hat x$ be any leader-feasible choice. Then
\[
  \lceil L-m\,p_{\max}\rceil\ \le\ \mathrm{OPT}\ \le\ \Phi(\hat x)\ \le\ U(\hat x).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every leader-feasible $x$, Lemma~\ref{lem:mkip-gap} gives
$\Phi(x)\ge U(x)-m\,p_{\max}(x)\ge U^{*}-m\,p_{\max}\ge L-m\,p_{\max}$, so
$\mathrm{OPT}\ge L-m\,p_{\max}$, and $\mathrm{OPT}$ is an integer because $p$ is.
The upper bounds hold because the leader may play $\hat x$ and the game is
zero-sum.
\end{proof}
\noindent Not formalised.

For the OR instances with one follower knapsack (variant $-100$, $m=1$) the
lower bound is $\lceil L-p_{\max}\rceil$, where $L$ is HiGHS's proven dual bound
for the MILP of Proposition~\ref{prop:mkip-milp} as the solver returns it (in
floating point, with no safety margin). The bracket's lower bound is therefore
solver-certified (class~(ii), Section~\ref{app:classes}); it is the reported
lower bound for \SuppOrLbMilp{} of the \NumOrOneKnapsack{} instances, and for
the other \SuppOrLbOmega{} the merged-row online bound below is at least as
strong. With several follower rows the width
$m\,p_{\max}$ makes the lower bound weak, and only the upper bounds are used.

\subsection{The merged-row online bound}

Theorem~\ref{thm:online} needs one leader row and one follower row. For one
follower row ($m=1$, weights $w_j=W_{1j}$, capacity $c=c_1$) and several leader
rows, \texttt{mkip::omega\_root} merges the leader rows into one surrogate row
and rescales both rows to small integers. Its inputs are multipliers
$\mu\in\mathbf{R}^{m_u}_{\ge 0}$ and scales $s_u,s_l>0$, all double-precision
numbers. For these numbers let
\[
  a_j=s_u\sum_{i=1}^{m_u}\mu_iG_{ij}\quad(j\in N),\qquad b=s_u\sum_{i=1}^{m_u}\mu_ig_i
\]
be the exact real values. The code computes them in IEEE double precision
(rounding to nearest): it forms the products $\mu_iG_{ij}$, adds them in the
order of the rows and multiplies the sum by $s_u$; we write $\hat a_j$ and
$\hat b$ for the computed values, and $\widehat{s_lw_j}$, $\widehat{s_lc}$ for
the computed products of the follower data with $s_l$. It then sets
\[
  \gamma'_j=\lfloor\hat a_j\rfloor,\qquad
  \beta'=\lceil\hat b\,\rceil,\qquad
  w'_j=\lceil\widehat{s_lw_j}\rceil,\qquad
  c'=\lfloor\widehat{s_lc}\rfloor,
\]
which are nonnegative integers for every $\mu\ge 0$ and $s_u,s_l>0$, since
all the data are nonnegative: leader costs and follower capacity are rounded
down, the leader budget and follower weights up. Finally it evaluates the
online game of Theorem~\ref{thm:online} with profits $p$, leader costs
$\gamma'$, budget $\beta'$, follower weights $w'$ and capacity $c'$ on the
list $o$ of the items with $p_j>0$ in decreasing order of $p_j/w_j$ (ties:
larger profit first), by the backward recursion of Section~\ref{app:kip} over
a table of $(\beta'+1)(c'+1)$ entries per item in 64-bit integer arithmetic.
Let $\Omega'=\Omega(o,\beta',c')$ be its value. Below,
$u=2^{-53}$ is the unit roundoff of double precision and
$\varepsilon_k=(1+u)^k-1$ (so $\varepsilon_k\le ku/(1-ku)$).

\begin{theorem}[Merged-row online bound]\label{thm:mkip-omega}
Let $p$, $G$, $g$, $w$ and $c$ be nonnegative integers, and $\mu\ge 0$,
$s_u,s_l>0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If the four rounded quantities are computed exactly
($\hat a_j=a_j$, $\hat b=b$, $\widehat{s_lw_j}=s_lw_j$, $\widehat{s_lc}=s_lc$), then
$\Omega'\le\mathrm{OPT}$.
\item[(b)] If every entry of $G$, $g$, $w$ and $c$ is below $2^{53}$ (so it
is a double), no product underflows, and
\[
  2\,\varepsilon_{m_u+1}\,b<1\qquad\text{and}\qquad 2uc<1-u,
\]
then $\Omega'\le\mathrm{OPT}$ for the data computed in double precision.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Two properties suffice.} Suppose the integer data satisfy
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(L)] $\gamma'(X)\le\beta'$ for every leader-feasible $X$ (that is,
$G\mathbf{1}_X\le g$), and
\item[(F)] $w(Y)\le c$ for every $Y\subseteq N$ with $w'(Y)\le c'$.
\end{enumerate}
By~(F) every packing for $(w',c')$ is a packing for $(w,c)$, so the best
packings satisfy $K'(T)\le K(T)$ for every $T\subseteq N$. Let $X$ be
leader-feasible. By~(L), $X$ fits the merged budget, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:online} for the data $(p,\gamma',\beta',w',c')$ gives
$\Omega'\le K'(o\setminus X)\le K(o\setminus X)\le K(N\setminus X)=\Phi(X)$.
Minimising over $X$ gives $\Omega'\le\mathrm{OPT}$. Both properties use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:merged-relax}
  a(X)=s_u\sum_i\mu_i(G\mathbf{1}_X)_i\le s_u\sum_i\mu_ig_i=b\qquad\text{for leader-feasible }X,
\end{equation}
which holds because $\mu\ge 0$ and $s_u>0$, and the fact that $w(Y)$ and $c$
are integers.

\emph{(a)} By~\eqref{eq:merged-relax},
$\gamma'(X)=\sum_{j\in X}\lfloor a_j\rfloor\le a(X)\le b\le\lceil b\rceil=\beta'$,
which is~(L). If $w'(Y)\le c'$, then
$s_lw(Y)\le\sum_{j\in Y}\lceil s_lw_j\rceil=w'(Y)\le c'=\lfloor s_lc\rfloor\le s_lc$,
so $w(Y)\le c$, which is~(F).

\emph{(b)} Each operation on doubles returns the exact result times a factor
in $[1-u,1+u]$, and every quantity involved is nonnegative. A term
$\mu_iG_{ij}$ of $a_j$ passes through at most $m_u+1$ roundings (its product,
at most $m_u-1$ additions, the final product with $s_u$; adding the first
term to zero is exact), so
$(1-\varepsilon_{m_u+1})a_j\le\hat a_j\le(1+\varepsilon_{m_u+1})a_j$, using
$1-(1-u)^k\le ku\le\varepsilon_k$; the same holds for $\hat b$ and $b$. The
follower's products have one rounding each:
$\widehat{s_lw_j}\ge(1-u)s_lw_j$ and $\widehat{s_lc}\le(1+u)s_lc$.
For~(L), let $X$ be leader-feasible. By~\eqref{eq:merged-relax},
$\gamma'(X)\le\sum_{j\in X}\hat a_j\le(1+\varepsilon_{m_u+1})\,a(X)\le(1+\varepsilon_{m_u+1})\,b$,
and $\beta'\ge\hat b\ge(1-\varepsilon_{m_u+1})\,b$. If $\gamma'(X)\ge\beta'+1$,
these inequalities give $2\varepsilon_{m_u+1}b\ge 1$, contrary to the
hypothesis; as both sides are integers, $\gamma'(X)\le\beta'$. For~(F), if
$w'(Y)\le c'$ then
$(1-u)\,s_lw(Y)\le\sum_{j\in Y}\widehat{s_lw_j}\le w'(Y)\le c'\le\widehat{s_lc}\le(1+u)\,s_lc$,
so $w(Y)\le c+2uc/(1-u)<c+1$, and $w(Y)\le c$ because both are integers.
\end{proof}
\noindent The online bound itself is formalised (Theorem~\ref{thm:online});
properties~(L) and~(F), the rounding analysis and the code are not.

The proof needs only the directions of the roundings, not the size of the
scales: lowering leader costs and raising the leader's budget enlarge the
leader's options in the relaxed game, raising follower weights and lowering
its capacity shrink the follower's options, and both moves can only lower the
value of the game. In exact arithmetic $\beta'=\lfloor b\rfloor$ would also
give~(L), because $\gamma'(X)$ is an integer; rounding the budget up leaves
the margin that part~(b) uses when $b$ is computed as a number just below an
integer.

What the code chooses affects only the strength of the bound.
\texttt{bobi bounds} starts from $\mu_i=1/g_i$ and improves $\mu$ by
coordinate ascent (\texttt{mkip::tune\_mu}: each $\mu_i$ in turn is
multiplied by $0$, $0.5$, $0.8$, $1.25$ or $2$ while the bound on a coarse
table improves; at most two sweeps). With its default of $4\cdot 10^6$ table
entries per item, \texttt{mkip::omega\_scales} gives $s_u=1000/\hat\sigma$,
where $\hat\sigma$ is the computed value of $\sum_i\mu_ig_i$, and $s_l=4000/c$;
so $b$ differs from $1000$ by less than $10^{-11}$, $\beta'\in\{1000,1001\}$
and $c'\in\{3999,4000\}$. In the \NumOrOneKnapsack{} OR instances with one
follower knapsack all data are integers (read from the instance files: the
largest coefficient is $1{,}366$ and the largest right-hand side $200{,}032$),
$m_u\le 29$ and no nonzero product comes near the underflow range, so
$2\varepsilon_{m_u+1}b<10^{-11}$ and $2uc<10^{-10}$: the hypotheses of
part~(b) hold with a wide margin, and every merged-row bound reported for
them is a valid lower bound in exact arithmetic: a mathematical proof plus an
integer computation, so an exact certificate (class~(i),
Section~\ref{app:classes}). It gives \SuppOrBetterLbOmega{} of the
\NumOrBetterLb{} lower bounds that improve on the published ones; the other
\SuppOrBetterLbMilp{} come from the LP bracket.

\subsection{Certified points}

\begin{proposition}[Certified point]\label{prop:mkip-point}
Let $\hat x$ satisfy $G\hat x\le g$, and let $\hat y$ be a follower response at
$\hat x$ proven optimal (by the exact knapsack oracle when $m=1$, or by a MILP
solver whose dual bound meets $p^{\top}\hat y$). Then $(\hat x,\hat y)$ is bilevel
feasible with value $\Phi(\hat x)$, so $\mathrm{OPT}\le\Phi(\hat x)$. Without an
exact follower solve, $\mathrm{OPT}\le\lfloor U(\hat x)\rfloor$ still holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\hat y\in S(\hat x)$ by definition; the game is zero-sum, so every element of
$S(\hat x)$ gives the leader the value $\Phi(\hat x)$. The last claim is
Lemma~\ref{lem:mkip-gap} and integrality of $\Phi$.
\end{proof}
\noindent Not formalised.

For several follower knapsacks the leader choice $\hat x$ is the minimiser
found for the MILP of Proposition~\ref{prop:mkip-milp}; when HiGHS proves the
follower's optimum at $\hat x$, the point is certified. For one follower
knapsack, a first-improvement local search on the leader's choice (add an
item the follower uses, or swap a blocked item for one it uses, keeping
every budget row) evaluates each candidate with the exact knapsack oracle, so
every incumbent it reports is a certified point. Every point reported is
checked again from its file by \texttt{bobi verify}
(Section~\ref{app:verification}). The data are integers and the points are
0/1 vectors, so the leader's rows hold exactly. With one follower knapsack the
follower's optimum comes from the exact knapsack oracle, which works in
integer arithmetic, and the \NumOrOneKnapsackPoints{} points are exact
certificates (class~(i), Section~\ref{app:classes}); with several, it rests on
HiGHS's dual bound, and the \NumOrSeveralPoints{} points are solver-certified
(class~(ii)). The follower LP bound $U(\hat x)$, an upper bound in the gaps of
Table~\ref{tab:or}, is a floating-point LP value (class~(ii)).

\subsection{Published bounds of the OR set}\label{app:mkip-or}

The paper compares this work's bounds with the published ones. The published
lower bounds of the OR instances are Lozano, Bergman and
Cire's;\cite{lozano2026network} the best published point of an instance is the
better of theirs (with a solution file in their repository\cite{lozano_dbo_repo})
and Lefebvre and Schmidt's.\cite{lefebvre2025dantzig} The published points are
not verified here. For \NumOrInconsistent{} instances a published lower bound
exceeds the best published point (Table~\ref{tab:or-conflicts}); the paper's
paired median gaps leave them out. In three of them the point is one of
Lefebvre and Schmidt's, from runs stopped at a time or solution limit, and it
lies below Lozano et al.'s lower bound; at least one of the two published
values is wrong, and the points of this work do not decide which.

The fourth, \texttt{\OrClosureName}, is closed in the published data. Lozano et
al.\ publish a point of value \OrClosureValue{} and a lower bound of
\OrClosureLitLb{} from runs they flag as numerically inconsistent; their
repository also records a branch-and-cut bound of \OrClosureValue{}, which with
the point proves the value optimal. The instance is therefore counted among the
published optima. This work's verified solution file at \OrClosureValue{}
(checked also with SCIP) confirms that the bound \OrClosureLitLb{} is wrong.
